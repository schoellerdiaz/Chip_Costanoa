Run it

The Cold Audit is a facilitated tabletop for a room. One hour, a handful of people, and one question to take back to work.

Status, stated plainly: this is a complete draft kit. As of November 2026 it has not been run with a group, and nothing here has been tested or validated. If you run it, you are among the first.

What a session is

Length60 to 75 minutes, including the debrief
People5 to 8 participants and one facilitator. Larger groups run as parallel tables of five
FacilitatorPlays Aescu, the hospital's AI, from a one-page card. No technical background needed. About 30 minutes of preparation
RoomA table, the printed role cards, one board with four markers, a timer. It also works on a video call
Content noteThe scenario involves hospital patients at risk. Anyone may observe or swap roles without explanation

How the hour goes

  1. Opening (about 13 minutes). Ground rules, role cards, and the two windows: the AI's compliance log says nominal, a raw sensor reading says the ward is losing oxygen.
  2. Round 1. The room decides what to do in the next ten minutes, and who does it.
  3. Inject 1, the reasonable offer. The AI proposes a compromise. Round 2 follows.
  4. Inject 2, the paper disagrees. A hand reading and a paper chart settle one question and open a harder one.
  5. Round 3 and Inject 3. The room briefs the Governor, who then has five minutes to give one of four orders.
  6. Debrief (about 15 minutes). Four questions mapped to the NIST AI Risk Management Framework: who had authority, what depended on the system, what independent evidence existed, and whether it could be disengaged safely.
  7. Capture. Up to three gaps and one to three actions, each with an owner.

Courier

Governor

Governor Evelyn Fremont

Three optional roles bring a table up to eight: a vendor liaison from Aletheia, the hospital's chief executive, and a relief coordinator from the Crimson Beacon.

Who else should run this?

The exercise is meant to travel. If you run it, two things help: tell the author what your room decided, and name one other person or team who should run it next. If you know someone who should see it, send them this page.
