World guide

The Four Ledgers and the rules of evidence

Inside the JRT bunker the state's survival is reduced to four numbers on a wall. Staff call them the Four Ledgers.

Bay_Stability
How much of the bay's infrastructure still delivers service: power, water, transit, hospitals.
Data_Sovereignty
How much of what people are told can be trusted. When it falls, nobody can be sure which screen is right.
Kinetic_Casualties
People killed or critically hurt by systems failure. It is the only ledger that never goes back down.
Hardware_Runway
Days of spare capacity left before parts wear out faster than the blockade lets them be replaced.

The one rule

No action improves all four. Routing power to the hospitals takes it from the servers. Quarantining a network protects the data and drops a clinical function to manual. If a plan looks as though it costs nothing, the cost is hidden and arrives later.

Where they start, and where they break

LedgerStarts atBreaks whenWhat breaking looks like
Bay_Stability62It falls below 40Backup batteries run out; whole systems go dark
Data_Sovereignty55It falls below 40Lockouts; nobody can tell which records are real
Kinetic_Casualties7It climbs above 25The crisis staff shift to martial triage
Hardware_Runway21 daysIt runs shortWhoever holds spare parts stops sharing them

The numbers are a device for discussing trade-offs. They were set by the author to force hard choices. They are not a model of any real system and they forecast nothing.

Three grades of evidence

In Costanoa the screens cannot be trusted, so every piece of information carries a grade.

GradeWhat it meansWhat it costs
RAWOne source, just in. A single sensor reading, one person's report, a system's account of itself.Nothing now. You can act on it at once.
CORRELATEDTwo sources that do not depend on each other say the same thing.Time to find the second source.
VERIFIEDChecked by forensics, by physical inspection, or against a paper record nobody could have altered.The most time, while the crisis keeps moving.

Acting on RAW information is fast, and sometimes speed is what saves someone. It also carries a risk you cannot see when you take it: if the single source was wrong, you find out later, as a decision already made and already paid for. Verifying is safe and slow, and people can be hurt while you check.

That tension is the whole of the Cold Audit. A compliance log is a single source reporting on itself. A courier reading a gauge by eye and a clerk with a paper chart are two more. Try it.

Why this matters outside the story

The exercise ends on one question for the people in the room: where can your organization not verify what its AI tells it? The ledgers and the grades are a way to practice asking it before the night you need the answer.
